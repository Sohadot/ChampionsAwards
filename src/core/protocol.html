Editorial protocol

The rules that make this a reference

Authority is not asserted; it is constrained into existence. These are the standards every entry is held to, published so they can be checked against the entries themselves.

1. Sourcing standard

Every substantive claim must be anchored to an independently checkable source, cited on the entry. Preference order: primary documents, then independent secondary scholarship, then official records. Self-descriptions and single-institution claims are marked as such. An entry that cannot meet this standard stays in draft and is not published.

2. Neutrality of language

Entries describe structure and evidence, not destiny. Superlatives ("greatest", "definitive", "sovereign-grade") are avoided in favor of scoped, falsifiable statements. The goal is a reader who trusts the reasoning, not one who is impressed by the tone.

3. Separation of score and opinion

A Deservingness Index score is only ever the output of the published formula applied to stated inputs. Editorial commentary is kept distinct from the score, so the number can be reproduced independently of anyone's view.

4. Conflict-of-interest disclosure

Any material relationship between the project and a subject, institution, or sponsor is disclosed on the relevant entry. Sponsorship, if it ever exists, never influences a score or an entry's inclusion.

5. Correction policy

Being wrong in public is treated as normal and fixable. Substantive corrections are logged with a date and a note on what changed, rather than silently overwritten. Readers can challenge any entry, and a well-sourced challenge is grounds for revision.

6. Methodology versioning

The scoring model is versioned (currently DDI v1.0). Any change to dimensions, weights, or bands increments the version and is dated, so a score always names the model that produced it.

7. What this project does not claim

It does not claim to deliver final judgment, to be exhaustive, or to be free of bias. It claims only to be transparent, source-anchored, reproducible, and correctable — and to be judged on whether it keeps those promises.
