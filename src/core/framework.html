Conceptual framework

Four ideas the whole platform rests on

The framework is deliberately small. If these four claims hold, the scoring and the archive follow; if they fail, the project should be argued out of them.

1. Merit and recognition are different things

Merit is the structural value of a contribution. Recognition is whether an institution chose to validate it publicly. Conflating the two is the core error the platform is built to avoid — and the reason a defined vocabulary comes before any score.

2. Awards are systems, not verdicts

A prize is an output of a selection process shaped by history, capital, narrative, and institutional continuity. Studying that process — its integrity, breadth, and blind spots — tells you more than the list of laureates does. That is the work of the recognition-systems cluster.

3. The gap is measurable

The distance between assessed merit and observed recognition can be estimated, consistently, with a published instrument. That is the Deservingness Index. The number matters less than the fact that it is produced the same way for everyone.

4. Omission is part of the record

A recognition system is defined as much by what it left out as by what it honored. The Unawarded Archive treats documented omission as primary evidence, not as trivia.
